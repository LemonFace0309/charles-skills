# charles-ux-animation

The 20 principles of UX and animation from my reel, as a procedure your agent walks every time it builds or reviews an interaction. It decides whether something should move at all, where the eye goes, how it moves, and whether people can hit it, choose it and read it.

1. Squash & stretch
2. Anticipation wind-up
3. Staging the focus
4. Pose to pose
5. Follow through
6. Slow in & slow out
7. Moving in arcs
8. Secondary action
9. Snappy timing
10. Exaggerated feedback
11. Solid drawing in 3D
12. Overall appeal
13. Fitts's law
14. Bigger hit areas
15. Hick's law
16. Miller's law
17. Doherty threshold
18. Postel's law
19. Jakob's law
20. Frequency of use

## Install

Run this in your project:

```bash
npx skills add LemonFace0309/charles-skills --skill charles-ux-animation
```

Add `-g` to install it for every project. Then start a new session and ask for it:

```text
/charles-ux-animation the add-to-cart flow
```

Codex uses `$charles-ux-animation` instead of the slash. To install by hand, and to see my other skills, read the [main README](../../README.md).

## Credits

1–12 are Disney's principles of animation from *The Illusion of Life* by Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston. Most of the list comes from Raphael Salaja's [userinterface.wiki](https://www.userinterface.wiki), the laws go deeper on Jon Yablonski's [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com), and #20 is Emil Kowalski's rule.
