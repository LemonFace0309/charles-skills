# Flip cards

The studio look on a card that flips in place: charles-explainers-assets' hack-reel card, one stage that shows a flaw, changes on my word and proves the fix. There's no Before tile and no After side, so the studio layer goes on the one stage. charles-explainers-assets ("Make it worth watching") has the motion rules; this page has what the look needs.

Gold, when the claude-editing repository is present: `videos/design-hacks/design-part-2/explainers/`. `scenes/src/00-stage.js` has the light and the pointers, and `DESIGN-LOG.md` has the rounds. charles-explainers-assets' `examples/gold-cards.jpg` shows the approved cards.

- **The interface stays, and the object is its cause.** A hack is about an interface (a button, a form, a toast), so the card shows that interface, as one sheet at a size where its words read. The object is the thing that does what the line is about:
  - a rubber stamp that marks a field INVALID at the first letter, for "don't show errors while people are typing";
  - a real Enter key that does nothing to a sign-in sheet;
  - a mouse that can hover, beside a phone that can't;
  - a toaster the toast pops out of;
  - paper receipts that a double click prints.

  Why: a white sheet over a dark pill on a grainy gradient is the screenshot-tool look. On design hacks part 2, the one card whose object caused the flaw scored 9 for interest where the sheet-only cards scored 4 to 5.
- **One key light for the whole card,** from the side the photos are lit from (the upper right): a big soft gradient from that corner to the deep colour at the far one, with every shadow thrown away from it. Never a spot of light behind a sheet. Why: the term card's spot lands on its object, but behind an interface it reads as one radial blob, a glow with no source, and behind a dark window as the AI default of a dark card on an indigo glow. Put a dark interface in daylight.
- **The move is the flaw and the fix,** not an entrance. The object acts to cause the flaw (it stamps, it's pressed, it pops and drops), and after my word the same object does the right thing: the stamp waits for the field to be left and then stamps VALID, the same key press signs in, the toast waits under the pointer. Keep it on screen through the fix. An object that leaves on my word is a second thing moving, and it empties a third of the card. Physics by material still holds: wood and plastic don't squash, paper and toast spring and settle.
- **Go forward at the change, and pay off on it.** Nothing resets or gets typed again on my word, and the thing a viewer would point at happens on the word itself: the stamp's red ink all goes, the duplicate receipt slides back in, the toast stops sinking. Why: a scene that rewinds on the word reads as the fix undoing the flaw's damage the wrong way, and a payoff a second late leaves a button greying or an outline appearing as the only change: a label for the fix, not the fix.
- **Make the object the timer, the counter or the meter** when the line has one. A toast that sinks into the toaster as its time runs out, and stops on my word, beats a thin progress bar on a toast that stands still: a thin bar stopping is too small a change to see on a phone.
- **Open at rest.** The editor cuts a flip in mid-clip, at the change minus my line's lead-in, and that lead-in is an estimate until I've filmed. Keep the scene in a clean pose from 0.3 s before that time to 0.3 s after it: no slip sliding back, no field clearing, no toast half-way down. Why: anything mid-move there reads as a loop seam, a glitch after a hard cut.
- **Keep the tag's corner clear.** The reel draws Before/After on the card's top-left corner, so nothing sits in 240 × 80 px there, at the end of the slow push too.
- **No two neighbours alike:** not the same hue, and not the same prop or product photo.
- **Two critics a round, and the frames round the change.**
  - A blind reader gets only the phone sheet and each card's frames 0.15 s apart, and says what goes wrong, what the fix teaches and how sure a phone viewer would be.
  - A craft critic gets the reference, the stills and every frame from 0.2 s before the change to 0.7 s after it, and scores "the change itself" beside interest, craft, tackiness and the phone read.

  Why: stills can't show a transition, and that's where "tacky" lives.
