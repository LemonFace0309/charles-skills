# The swipe file: 22 real posters

Quartr posts a poster before big companies' earnings calls. The ones below are the most liked and most shared, mostly
from the 2026 run (picked 19 Nov 2026). They're by Oliver Hamrin (Quartr co-founder), Haris Cehic and Jacob Titus, who
say they use Figma and Midjourney: "a mix of photos, vector, overlays, and AI-generated imagery". Their influences:
album and book covers, film stills, old adverts, fine art, nature photography.

Get the images with `bun <kit>/poster.mjs refs` (they download to `~/.cache/charles-create-posters/refs/`, with a
labelled `sheet.jpg`). Open the sheet, then at full size the three or four posters closest to the medium you're
heading for. They're Quartr's work: study them, never copy one, never give one to an image model as a reference, and
never redistribute them.

What you're taking from them is the picture-making: where the idea comes from, what it's made in, and how much is
left out. The type on them is Quartr's earnings format around Quartr's wordmark; that stays theirs.

## Read every poster as one move

Each poster is one move: a way of turning the company's own world into a single picture. The idea always comes from
the company, never from finance. The ids match the files in the refs folder.

### The name, made literal

- **07 BlackRock, Q3 2026.** A pixel-art mountain: a black rock under a red sky. Six flat colours inside a white
  page; the copy runs up the left edge. The pun is childish on purpose and the medium makes it charming, not corny.
- **09 Dollar Tree, Q2 2026.** A tree whose leaves are green price tags ($1.25, $1.50, $3.00) and whose trunk is
  brown ones, a collage of real stickers on dark green. The prices on the tags tell the business story.
- **15 Robinhood, Q3 2025.** A fox Robin Hood drawing his bow in an oval frame, an antique engraving printed green on
  green bookcloth. The most liked of the 2025 run (3.8K).

### The logo, made real

- **20 Ferrari, Q3 2025.** The prancing horse as a real black horse rearing in deep red shadow. One colour, two values.
- **21 Apple, Q4 2025.** A red apple whose flesh is packed with circuit boards, on Apple's own pale grey.
- **13 Meta, Q4 2025.** A classical marble bust wearing Meta's infinity logo as VR goggles, drawn in one blue marker
  stroke; the copy runs along the loop. Old against new, in two colours.
- **12 Alphabet, Q4 2025.** Sundar Pichai in black-and-white halftone wearing a crown of Gemini's four-point sparkles.
  Colour only where the idea is.

### The founder's signature

- **01 NVIDIA, Q3 FY2027.** Jensen Huang's leather jacket hangs alone on a coat rack in a dark room, small and low in
  a black frame. 4.5K likes, 126K views. The making-of below is the most useful thing in this file.
- **18 Palantir, Q3 2025.** Alex Karp's hair, his face lost in a white blur crossed by a band of half-legible text.
  Recognisable from the hair alone; Oliver thinks this one may have started the viral run.

### The product, staged as fine art

- **06 Tesla, Q3 2026.** A silver Tesla flips through the dark trailing rocket smoke: the quarter staged as a launch
  (Musk's other company, and the promise that the cars will fly). Oliver posted four takes; clean pure black, no grain.
- **19 McDonald's, Q3 2025.** Three burgers tumbling through the dark in slow motion over one hard shadow: the world's
  cheapest food shot like a luxury campaign.
- **16 Dole, Q3 2025.** A banana plant at night, flash-lit, one yellow bunch among dark leaves. Oliver's favourite.
- **08 Porsche, Capital Markets Day 2026.** A line of identical blue 911s curving across white like a racing line or a
  chart. Repetition turns a car photo into a graphic and leaves a clean bay for the copy.

### The words they're known for

- **11 Meta, Q2 2026.** "Move fast and break things" bursts from the centre like shattered glass over a pixel map in
  Meta blue with one magenta accent. The motto does what it says.
- **10 Palantir, Q2 2026.** "SOFTWARE THAT DOMINATES" typed over and over in monospace, with Palantir's ring ghosted
  out of the text; the title runs large and vertical up the left edge.
- **22 Uber, Q3 2025.** "So like how long you been doing Uber?" over a motion-blurred back-seat view of city lights.
  The line every rider has said.

### The thing they own, or the thing they fight

- **04 Verisign, Q3 2026.** A wall of ".com" in twenty typefaces, yellow on black inside a yellow frame. Verisign runs
  the .com registry; the type specimen is the picture.
- **03 Rollins, Q3 2026.** Cockroaches scattered across signal red (Rollins owns Orkin), one crawling over the copy.
  Gross, made beautiful.

### The heritage

- **05 Hermès, Q3 2026 trading update.** A tiny horse-drawn carriage on loose red watercolour checks, from the house's
  harness-making roots; the paint stops above an unpainted paper band that holds the info. One of twelve variants.
- **14 Caterpillar, Q4 2025.** A torn-paper collage of excavators and quarries in Cat yellow, brown and black.

### The medium is the message

- **17 Figma, Q3 2025.** A Figma canvas holding Quartr's own posters, with multiplayer cursors saying "plz fix",
  "crisp" and "thanks bruv". Oliver called it the cleverest concept of the run.

### The brand colour as the whole picture

- **02 Nu Holdings, Q3 2026.** Nu's purple (#820AD1) in four tints plus near-black, broken into pixels that drip down
  from the top and rise from the bottom; the info stacks in the quiet middle. No photo at all.

## What the run has in common

- **One subject or one pattern.** Never two ideas. The compositions vary on purpose: a small subject in a big quiet
  field (NVIDIA, Tesla), a big subject filling the frame (Ferrari, Apple, Alphabet), a full-bleed pattern (Rollins,
  Nu, Dollar Tree), type as the picture (Verisign, Palantir). The info always gets a quiet bay.
- **The brand's own colours and nothing else.** Nu purple, Ferrari red, Cat yellow, NVIDIA's black, Meta blue.
- **Almost no type.** Theirs is a lockup and two grey sentences about the call at one small size; yours is the logo
  alone unless the user asks for words or an event. No headline, except when the words are the art (Uber's quote,
  Meta's motto) or a sideways title (Palantir).
- **Finished like print.** Film grain and dust on photos, paper tooth on flat colour, pure black only on clean CGI.
- **The medium fits the company.** Painterly for Hermès, pixels for a digital bank, type for a domain registry,
  engraving for a folk hero, macro food photography for McDonald's.
- **A wink, never a sneer.** Every one is something the company's own fans would share.

## How the NVIDIA poster was made (the making-of)

Haris posted the team's sheet of about 25 versions with their notes on each
([x.com/haris_chc/status/2122813105424211981](https://x.com/haris_chc/status/2122813105424211981)), and Oliver posted
15 of the rejects at full size ([x.com/oliverhamrin/status/2123037799339495657](https://x.com/oliverhamrin/status/2123037799339495657));
`refs` doesn't download these, so open the links. The story that quarter was talk of Jensen Huang's succession.

| Version | The team's note |
| --- | --- |
| The jacket on a coat rack, lit | "right idea, not there yet" |
| A blurred figure | "doesn't feel right" |
| An eBay listing of the jacket | "the runner-up. a bit funny but not it" |
| "All eyes on Kress" (the CFO, in a remake of their viral Huang poster) | "the story is still Jensen" |
| A Help Wanted ad: "looking for a new CEO?" | "nah..." |
| Jensen in the dark | "this is more it" |
| Seven Jensen variations: spirals, echoes, halftone, an eye strip | "not bad", "almost the vibe", "too video game?", "blurry??", "no lines?", "just his eyes was kind of good" |
| A pixel grid | "pure graphic design perhaps? not the vibe" |
| Jensen and his CFO blurred into one face | "perhaps we should show the transition?" |
| A blurred Jensen behind a handwritten "if the board decides my return..." letter | "oh boy jensen just tweeted?" |
| Jensen standing small at the edge of a black frame, "The end of an era..?" | "this is even more it. how can we improve it?" |
| The jacket alone on its rack in the dark | "keep just the jacket. leaving or coming back? this is the one." |

What it teaches:

1. **The story picks the subject.** They tried the CFO, a want ad and an eBay listing, and came back to the one person
   the news was about.
2. **A quiet metaphor beats a literal joke.** The eBay listing and the want ad were funny and lost.
3. **Effects aren't ideas.** The spirals, echoes and pixel grids are all well made and all rejected: "too video game".
4. **Subtract until the picture asks a question.** The winner takes Jensen out of the picture. The jacket says who;
   the empty room says "leaving or coming back?"
5. **Make many.** Even this team needed about 25 versions, and craft was never the problem: the idea was.
